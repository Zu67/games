# Games

## Dungeon Delver

This repository is Dungeon Delver, a terminal roguelike. It does not contain Headcount or a five-city population game. `main.py` opens with "Dungeon Delver — a terminal roguelike. Five floors, one life." The title screen says "one chance," not "one life." `game/__init__.py` is empty. `Enemy` has no defense field. `player_damage` adds the roll to attack and does not subtract anything else. `CLAUDE.md` says `ui.py` never changes player or enemy state, and that file only reads those fields. The same note lists poison, a shop room, and save files as things to add. None of those are in the game. You pick a name, then descend five floors. Floors 1 through 4 are three fights, one treasure room, and one rest, in a random order. Floor 5 is a rest and then the dragon. Floors other than 5 build the list `[COMBAT, COMBAT, TREASURE, REST, COMBAT]` and shuffle it, so the three fights are the same room type and only the order changes. `random.shuffle` permutes the five entries uniformly. The three fights are the same type, so those 120 permutations are 20 distinct layouts, and each layout has probability 1/20. The treasure room is equally likely in any of the five positions, and so is the rest. The first room is a fight with probability 3/5. Each room then picks its own line, so two fights on one floor can show the same sentence. A fight has 4 lines and each fight draws independently, so the three draws have 64 equally likely results. 24 of them use three different lines, which is 3/8. 4 of them use the same line three times, which is 1/16. The other 36, 9/16, use exactly two different lines. Floor 5's rest picks one of the three rest lines. The dragon line is the only boss line, and it is taken by index rather than by a random choice. `generate_floor` uses that five-room list for every floor number other than 5. The game only asks for 1 through 5. Combat rooms use one of four lines: "Torchlight flickers across crude weapon racks lining the walls.", "The stench of blood and decay hits you as you enter.", "Bones crunch beneath your boots in this grim chamber.", or "Crude carvings of monsters mark the damp stone walls." Treasure rooms use one of three: "A dusty chest sits enticingly in the center of the room.", "Something glints in the rubble near the far wall.", or "A skeleton slumps over a small pile of valuables." Rest rooms use one of three: "A surprisingly cozy alcove with a bedroll and a fresh torch.", "A shrine radiates warmth — your wounds begin to close.", or "Clear water trickles from a crack in the wall." The dragon's room always says "The ground trembles as you step into this vast, echoing chamber."

The name is stripped, so a blank name, including spaces only, becomes Hero. You start at 30 HP, attack 8, defense 3, level 1, 0 gold, and 2 potions. The next level costs 20 XP. The HP bar is 20 cells and the XP bar is 15. Filled cells are `█` and empty cells are dim `░`. The attack number is red and the defense number is blue. The fill is `int(current / maximum * width)`, so a fraction of a cell is dropped. The color is green when the ratio is above one half, yellow when it is above one quarter, and red at one quarter and below. A maximum of 0 draws an empty red bar. At 30 HP, 16 through 30 are green, 8 through 15 are yellow, and 0 through 7 are red. 15 HP and 16 HP both fill 10 cells. 1 HP fills none, the same as 0. At 20 XP, 11 through 20 are green, 6 through 10 are yellow, and 0 through 5 are red. 1 XP fills none. The enemy bar uses those same 20 cells. A goblin is red at 0 through 3, yellow at 4 through 6, and green at 7 through 12. A skeleton is red at 0 through 3, yellow at 4 through 7, and green at 8 through 15. An orc is red at 0 through 5, yellow at 6 through 11, and green at 12 through 22. 11 and 12 both fill 10 cells. A cave troll is red at 0 through 8, yellow at 9 through 17, and green at 18 through 35. The dragon is red at 0 through 15, yellow at 16 through 30, and green at 31 through 60. 15 and 16 both fill 5 cells, and 30 and 31 both fill 10. A goblin or skeleton at 1 HP still fills one cell. An orc or cave troll at 1 HP fills none, and the dragon fills none at 1 or 2 HP.

Your hit is attack plus a roll from -2 to +3, and it never falls below 1. At attack 8 and defense 3, that hit is 6 through 11. A goblin hits for 1 through 6, a skeleton for 2 through 7, an orc for 5 through 10, a cave troll for 8 through 13, and the dragon for 13 through 18. A 12 HP goblin survives one of those hits and dies on the second, including two hits of 6. An enemy hit is that enemy's attack plus the same roll, then defense is subtracted once, and it also never falls below 1. That floor is inside `enemy_damage`. The fight does not call `take_damage`. `main.py` subtracts the returned number from `player.hp` with `max(0, player.hp - dmg)`. `take_damage` has the same floor, and only the tests call it. With defense 3, `take_damage(4)`, `take_damage(3)`, `take_damage(0)`, and `take_damage(-10)` each return 1 and leave 29 HP. `take_damage(5)` returns 2 and leaves 28 HP. A potion heals 15 HP, up to your maximum. At full HP the potion is still used, the heal is 0, and the message says you have no potions left. A rest heals 15 HP and does not use a potion. Treasure is a potion when `random.random()` is below 0.4, so that is 2/5 of the time. Otherwise the gold is `random.randint(5, 20)` times the floor. That roll is inclusive, so it is one of 16 amounts, 5 through 20, each with probability 1/16 given that the room pays gold. The mean roll is 12.5. A treasure room on floor f therefore returns 7.5f gold on average, and the four rooms on floors 1 through 4 return 75 gold on average. Winning a fight adds that enemy's XP and gold. A fight calls `gain_xp` once, so it can raise your level only once. Leftover XP stays, and the next level costs the old requirement times 1.6, rounded down. That leftover can already meet the new requirement. A dragon's 50 XP added to 19 XP leaves 49 toward a requirement of 32, and the next `gain_xp` call levels you again, including a call that adds 0. Until that call, the 15-cell XP bar is drawn from 49/32. It is green and 22 cells long, and the empty portion is not drawn because `width - filled` is negative. At a requirement of 32, 34 XP still fills 15 cells and 35 XP fills 16. A level-up adds 10 max HP, refills HP, and adds 3 attack and 1 defense. Reaching level 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 takes 20, 52, 103, 184, and 313 XP in total. The requirement after each of those is 32, 51, 81, 129, and 206. Three won combats on each of floors 1 through 4, plus the dragon, and no flee, award 173 XP at the minimum enemy on every floor and 275 at the maximum. 173 XP reaches level 4 with 70 XP toward the next 81. 275 XP reaches level 5 with 91 XP toward the next 129. The enemies on a full clear are independent, and there are 46,656 equally likely lineups. Their average XP is 220, which is level 5 with 36 XP toward the next 129. 46,503 of those lineups reach level 5, which is 5,167/5,184. The other 153 stay at level 4, which is 17/5,184. The only award that can cross two requirements in one `gain_xp` call is the dragon's 50, and only from the first requirement of 20. A full clear already has at least 123 XP before the dragon, so that call does not bank a second level, and the final level matches the XP total. Those 123 to 225 XP are already awarded before the dragon fight, because floor 5 is a rest and then the boss. The average is 170, which is level 4 with 67 XP toward the next 81. 37,919 of the 46,656 lineups enter that fight at level 4. The other 8,737 enter at level 5. None are still level 3. Level 4 has 60 max HP, attack 17, and defense 6. The attack roll is then 15 through 20, each 1/6, and the floor of 1 does not apply. The dragon's roll minus that defense is 10 through 15. Only three 20s kill the dragon in 3 hits, which is 1/216. Every other attack sequence kills on the 4th hit, which is 215/216. The expected number of attacks, if you only attack, is 863/216. Level 5 has 70 max HP, attack 20, and defense 7. The attack roll is 18 through 23. The dragon's roll minus that defense is 9 through 14. 160 of the 216 three-hit sequences kill, which is 20/27, and the other 7/27 kill on the 4th hit. The expected number of attacks is 88/27. The rest before the dragon heals 15 HP and does not fill the bar, so current HP can be below the maximum. The same clear awards 87 gold at every enemy minimum and 245 at every enemy maximum. The four treasure rooms are on floors 1 through 4. Each is a potion 40% of the time, so those rooms can add no gold. When every one pays, the added gold is 50 through 200, and the combined maximum is 445.

It breathes fire." An unknown floor number would draw a Goblin. The draw is `random.choice` on that floor's list. Floor 1 is Goblin or Skeleton, each 1/2. Floor 2 is Goblin, Skeleton, or Orc Warrior, each 1/3. Floor 3 is Orc Warrior, Skeleton, or Cave Troll, each 1/3. Floor 4 is Orc Warrior or Cave Troll, each 1/2. Floor 5 is the Ancient Dragon. The fallback list contains only the Goblin factory. The game only asks for floors 1 through 5. The gold cell is filled by `random.randint` when the enemy is created, before the first round. Each range in the table is inclusive and uniform: a goblin has 6 amounts and a mean of 5.5, a skeleton has 6 and a mean of 7.5, an orc has 8 and a mean of 11.5, a troll has 9 and a mean of 16, and the dragon has 21 and a mean of 40. Winning every fight, using the floor odds above, returns 19.5 gold from floor 1, 24.5 from floor 2, 35 from floor 3, 41.25 from floor 4, and 40 from the dragon. That is 160.25 gold. A flee pays none of the enemy's gold. The treasure rooms are separate, so their 75 expected gold is on top of that, and a full clear averages 235.25 gold.

A hit logs "⚔️  You strike {name} for {dmg} damage!". A potion that heals logs "🧪 You drink a potion and recover {healed} HP!". A potion that returns 0 logs "You have no potions left!". A successful flee logs "💨 You slip back through the doorway!" and returns `"flee"` before the enemy hits. `run_combat` is documented to return `"win"`, `"lose"`, or `"flee"`. `handle_room` checks only `"lose"`. A win and a flee both continue to the next room. The line after the fight loop, `return "win" if player.is_alive() else "lose"`, is not reached. A kill returns `"win"` inside the loop, a death returns `"lose"` inside the loop, and the loop itself continues only while both are alive. A failed flee logs "Your escape route is cut off!", and the enemy still hits. That hit logs "💥 {name} strikes you for {dmg} damage!". If the player's hit drops the enemy to 0, the enemy does not strike in that round. A round that leaves both alive waits on "Press Enter to continue...". A death or a kill does not. That sentence is the round prompt. A kill then opens the defeat panel, which waits on "Press Enter to continue..." on its own. `show_game_over` and `show_victory` call `console.clear()` and do not wait, so the process ends on that screen. Treasure waits on "You cautiously approach... Press Enter." and the panel then waits again on "Press Enter to continue...". Rest waits on "You take a moment to rest... Press Enter." and the panel then waits again on the same continue line. Ctrl-C prints "Farewell, adventurer." and exits 0. Fleeing a fight, including the dragon, does not end the run. The next room still happens. Leaving floor 5, even by fleeing the dragon, is the win. The title banner is "⚔️   D U N G E O N   D E L V E R   ⚔️", with a space between each letter and three spaces between the two words. The line under it still says "A roguelike adventure — five floors, one chance." The stats panel rows are "Hero", "HP", "XP", "ATK / DEF", "Gold", and "Potions". The game code is `main.py` and `game/`. Tests are in `tests/`. They never import `main.py`, so they do not run a fight, a flee, or a room. `test_take_damage_subtracts_defense` expects `take_damage(8)` with defense 3 to return 5 and leave 25 HP. `test_take_damage_minimum_one` expects `take_damage(5)` with defense 100 to return 1 and leave 29 HP. `test_gain_xp_triggers_level_up` checks that `gain_xp(20)` returns true, the level is 2, and HP equals max HP. It does not check that max HP is 40, attack is 11, or defense is 4. The combat tests patch `random.randint` and check only `player_damage` and `enemy_damage`. `test_use_potion_no_potions_returns_zero` checks only that the return value is 0. It does not check HP or that the potion count stays 0. `test_gain_xp_no_level_up` checks that the level stays 1 and does not check that XP is 5. `test_is_alive` sets HP to 0 by assignment. It does not call `take_damage`. `test_heal_caps_at_max_hp` starts at 20 HP with a maximum of 30, calls `heal(20)`, and expects 10 HP healed and 30 HP remaining. No test checks that one `gain_xp` call levels only once. There are 12 tests, 8 in `tests/test_player.py` and 4 in `tests/test_combat.py`. `requirements.txt` asks for `rich>=13.0` and `pytest>=8.0`. The combat helper builds an enemy named Goblin with gold 5 and description "test". `test_player_damage_minimum_one` uses attack 1 and a roll of -2 and expects 1. `test_player_damage_uses_attack_stat` uses attack 10 and a roll of 0 and expects 10. `test_enemy_damage_reduced_by_defense` uses defense 4, enemy attack 10, and a roll of 0, and expects 6. `test_enemy_damage_minimum_one` uses defense 100, enemy attack 5, and a roll of 0, and expects 1. `test_use_potion_heals_and_consumes` starts at 10 HP, a maximum of 30, and 2 potions. It expects 15 HP healed, 25 HP left, and 1 potion left. `test_is_alive` starts at 1 HP and expects true before it assigns 0.
